# Responsible use

ReversePort exists to understand and move **your own** software and games — and code you are allowed to work on —
between engines and languages.

Good uses:

- Porting your own game or app to another engine, platform or language.
- Understanding, documenting and refactoring codebases you maintain or inherited.
- Recovering your own assets or scripts from your own builds when the source was lost.
- Comparing your builds to see what changed.
- Interoperability, research, preservation, and modding where the game's license or the law allows it.

What ReversePort does not do, by design:

- It does not decrypt encrypted packs, bypass DRM, licence checks, anti-cheat, code signing or any other protection.
  Encrypted content is reported as encrypted and left alone.
- It does not patch or modify the programs it inspects. Analysis is read-only; output goes to separate folders.
- Its skills and agent instructions tell AI agents to refuse those uses too.

You are responsible for having the rights to what you analyse and port. When in doubt, check the license or ask the
owner. Copyrighted assets stay copyrighted after a port.
